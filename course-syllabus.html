What the revision fixes

CYBER 207 remains the modeling course: six weeks of classical ML, a pre-midterm integration lab, a midterm, three focused weeks of deep learning, and two LLM sessions. CYBER 290 remains the AI security course, avoiding repeated coverage of prompt attacks, AI red teaming, secure deployment, governance, and agent security.

Methods before systems

Regression, classification, trees, features, metrics, clustering, and dimensionality reduction receive dedicated treatment.

Security throughout

Every method is taught through a cyber decision, threat model, and operational cost.

Two LLM sessions

Transformers, prompting, RAG, fine-tuning, and evaluation, without duplicating AI security material from CYBER 290.

Evidence over fluency

Students measure task outcomes, failure modes, and harm rather than aesthetic output.

The 14-week teaching sequence

This sequence stays close to the original agenda. Every 90-minute class combines explanation, a worked cybersecurity example, a short exercise, and interpretation of results.

Course boundary: 207 versus 290

The courses may use some of the same model vocabulary, but they answer different questions. CYBER 207 asks how models learn and how to evaluate their output. CYBER 290 asks how AI systems fail under attack and how to secure and govern them.

207 · Classical ML

Regression, optimization, KNN, trees, ensembles, feature engineering, logistic regression, multiclass metrics, k-means, and PCA.

207 · Deep learning

Feedforward networks, backpropagation, embeddings, CNNs, attention, transformers, and model selection.

207 · LLM methods

Pretraining intuition, prompting, structured output, RAG, fine-tuning choices, and task evaluation.

290 · Threats

Adversarial attacks, model abuse, LLM vulnerability classes, malicious prompting, and offensive assessment.

290 · Secure architecture

Zero-trust AI design, identity and access controls, privacy techniques, defense in depth, and secure deployment.

290 · Operations & governance

MLSecOps, AI agents in security operations, organizational risk, compliance, and governance frameworks.

Four projects at the original milestones

feature pipeline→unsupervised analysis→deep model→LLM method study

Project 1 · Week 3

Build a reproducible security feature pipeline and compare classical baselines without leakage.

Project 2 · Week 6

Use clustering and dimensionality reduction to investigate campaigns, then explain limitations.

Project 3 · Week 11

Train and evaluate a deep model for text, bytes, or malware images against a classical baseline.

Project 4 · Week 13

Compare prompting, retrieval, and adaptation for one bounded task using a documented evaluation set.

Assessment architecture

15%

Weekly evidence briefs

Short analyses connecting model results to assumptions, data quality, metrics, and operational decisions.

20%

Midterm practical

Build, evaluate, and critique a classical ML detector under imbalance and temporal shift.

35%

Four scaffolded projects

Feature pipeline, unsupervised analysis, deep model, and LLM method comparison.

30%

Final project

End-to-end modeling study with baselines, evaluation, failure analysis, demo, and defense.

Reading strategy

  • Foundation: Chollet chapters already assigned in the original agenda for tensors, optimization, generalization, text, and convolutional networks.
  • Classical methods: Existing RM selections for linear models, trees, ensembles, unsupervised learning, and metrics.
  • Cyber intuition: Deep Learning for Cyber, especially neural networks, training, text embeddings, malware as images, and sequence models.
  • LLM methods: Current primary material on transformers, retrieval, fine-tuning choices, and evaluation, refreshed each term.
